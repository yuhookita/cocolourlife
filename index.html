CoColour Life

Connecting research and practice for more accessible and sustainable health and rehabilitation.

About

CoColour Life Pty Ltd is an Australian company established in 2026 to support the responsible translation of research into practice, education and collaboration in health and rehabilitation. We seek to contribute to more accessible and sustainable health and rehabilitation services, with a particular focus on Australia–Japan collaboration. Current areas of activity include research and evaluation, professional education, and selected advisory and collaborative projects.

CoColour Life Pty Ltd is a private Australian company. Relevant professional and commercial interests are disclosed and managed in accordance with applicable organisational, contractual and professional requirements.

Our name

“Co” is how we work: collaboration, co-design and growing together. Nothing meaningful is created alone.

“Colour” is what we work for: everyday lives rich in the occupations that make each person who they are; services and collaboration that reach people of diverse cultures and backgrounds; and the conviction that we do not add colour to people’s lives — we create colour together.

The circles in our logo — each a different size and colour — represent the everyday activities and the many different people we work with. It is when they connect that life gains its colour. The large red circle on the right is drawn from the Japanese flag: a mark of our roots in Japan as we build connections from Australia and beyond.

In Japanese, CoColour sounds like kokkara — “from here.”

Life starts from here.

Areas of activity

  • Research and evaluation Program and service evaluation, health workforce and cost analysis, and implementation research. Evaluation work is delivered as a report setting out what worked for the people using the service, what did not, and what the evidence does not yet cover.
  • Education and knowledge translation Lectures, workshops and teaching materials for health professionals and students, in English and Japanese. Existing material is rebuilt for the setting where it will be used rather than translated as it stands.
  • Advisory and collaborative projects Advisory work with health services, universities and industry: shaping a project before it starts, or reviewing one already running. The number we take on at a time is limited.
  • Australia–Japan collaboration in health and rehabilitation Exchange of evidence, models of care and technology in both directions. Most of the effort goes into working out what has to change for something that works in one country to work under the funding and service arrangements of the other.

Examples of past enquiries

  • Taking a product or service into another country.A company or research group with a healthcare product or service already in use in one country, looking to introduce it in another. What they usually need first is a clear account of the evidence they will be asked for, and of the conditions in the setting where it would be used.
  • Introducing a way of working to the other country.A practitioner or organisation that has seen a way of working take hold in Australia or in Japan, and wants to bring it to the other country with colleagues there rather than on their own. In practice this often means joint presentations, co-authored writing, and rebuilding existing material together.
  • Setting up an international project or study.Researchers or clinicians with an international project or study in mind, who know the question they want to ask but not how a collaboration across two systems is set up and kept going. We can answer some of this from experience. Some of it we work out together.

Not every enquiry is a fit. Where it is not, we say so, and, where we can, we point to someone better placed.

Founder

Founder and Director: Yuho Okita

Yuho Okita is an occupational therapist registered in Australia and a researcher based in Melbourne. His work spans digital health, rehabilitation, health workforce research, and professional education, and he contributes to university research and education in Australia and Japan. He founded CoColour Life to help research findings reach everyday health and rehabilitation practice. He continues to maintain a small clinical practice.
